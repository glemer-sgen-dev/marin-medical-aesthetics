## §2 the problem it is aimed at (M)

**Section heading:** What people come in with

Leaking with a cough, a laugh, a sneeze or a lift. Sudden urgency and not always getting there.
Pelvic-floor weakness after childbirth, surgery or menopause, and the daily planning that builds up
around it.

Most people have already tried pelvic-floor exercises, often for a long time, and either could not
tell whether they were doing them correctly or found they did not go far enough.

## §3 how this practice handles it (M)

**Section heading:** How treatment here is decided

- **The evaluation covers function as well as appearance.** Continence and pelvic-floor strength are
  assessed in the same appointment as everything else, not treated as a specialist add-on.
- **It ends in a written plan you keep.** What was assessed, what is recommended, and in what order.
- **It decides whether a treatment is appropriate at all.** For some presentations the answer is a
  referral rather than a device, and that is a real result of the appointment.

An evaluation determines whether a treatment is appropriate at all. It is not a safety or outcome
guarantee.

---

## §4 what the treatment involves (S)

**Section heading:** What a session is like

You sit, fully clothed, on the device for the session. It produces contractions in the pelvic-floor
muscles well beyond what voluntary effort achieves. There is no undressing, no catheter, no
anaesthetic and no recovery period, so you can return to the rest of your day afterwards.

It is delivered as a course rather than a single session. The number and interval are set at the
evaluation.

This practice's patients are typically adults roughly 35-65 across Marin County who want visible

## §6 what this does not replace (S)

**Section heading:** What this is not

- **Not a substitute for medical investigation.** Pain, blood, or a sudden change in symptoms need
  proper assessment first, and the evaluation will say so.
- **Not a cure**, and not a guarantee that leaking stops.
- **Not surgery**, and not presented as an alternative to a surgical repair where that is what is
  indicated.
- **Not a replacement for pelvic-floor exercise**, which usually remains part of the plan.

## §8 questions people actually ask (M)

**Section heading:** Common questions

**Do I have to undress?**
No. You remain fully clothed and seated throughout.

**What does it feel like?**
Strong contractions and a tingling sensation. It is not described as painful, though it is unfamiliar
for the first minutes.

**How many sessions will I need?**
It is a course. The number and spacing are set at your evaluation rather than advertised in advance,
because they depend on what is found.

**Is there any downtime?**
None is expected. People generally return to normal activity immediately.

**Who is it not suitable for?**
There are contraindications, including certain implanted metal and electronic devices, and pregnancy.
Screening for those is part of the evaluation.

**What does it cost?**
Quote-only pricing. During your consultation, we will build a customized treatment plan with packaged
pricing.
