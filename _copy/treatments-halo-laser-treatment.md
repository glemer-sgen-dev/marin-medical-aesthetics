---

## §2 the problem it is aimed at (M)

**Section heading:** What people come in with

Skin where several things have changed at once: tone is uneven, texture is rough, pores are more
visible, and years of sun are showing. Individually none of it is dramatic; collectively it reads as
tired.

This is usually the point at which gentler treatments have produced some improvement but not enough.

---

## §3 how this practice handles it (M)

**Section heading:** How treatment here is decided

- **The evaluation covers function as well as appearance**, and includes whether your skin and your
  schedule suit a treatment with real recovery.
- **It ends in a written plan you keep**, including what the recovery week actually looks like.
- **It decides whether a treatment is appropriate at all**, and for some skin types and some pigment
  patterns the answer here is no.

An evaluation determines whether a treatment is appropriate at all. It is not a safety or outcome
guarantee.

---

## §4 what the treatment involves (S)

**Section heading:** What a session is like

Numbing cream first, then the laser is passed over the treatment area. Skin is hot and red
afterwards, and over the following days it darkens, roughens and flakes before revealing the new
surface underneath.

That sequence is normal, it is not a complication, and you are told about it in detail before you
book rather than discovering it on day three.

## §6 what this does not do (S)

**Section heading:** What this is not

- **Not a no-downtime treatment.** There is a visible recovery period and it is stated plainly.
- **Not suitable for every skin type or every pigment pattern.** In melasma in particular, resurfacing
  can worsen the problem.
- **Not a facelift.** It changes skin surface and quality, not structure or laxity.
- No "typical results" framing and no outcome guarantee.

## §8 questions people actually ask (M)

**Section heading:** Common questions

**How much downtime is there?**
Several days of visible recovery for most people. The specifics are set at your evaluation, and if
your schedule cannot accommodate it, that is worth knowing before booking rather than after.

**Does it hurt?**
Numbing cream is used. The sensation is described as hot and prickling. It is not called painless.

**Can I have it if I have melasma?**
That is exactly the question the evaluation answers. Resurfacing can aggravate melasma, so the
assessment comes first.

**How many treatments?**
Fewer than a gentle laser, sometimes one, sometimes a small series. It is set at the evaluation.

**What does it cost?**
Quote-only pricing. During your consultation, we will build a customized treatment plan with packaged
pricing.
