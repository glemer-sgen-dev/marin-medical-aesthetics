## §1 hero (S)

**H1:** Melasma in San Anselmo

Patchy, often symmetrical darkening across the cheeks, forehead or upper lip. It is the pigment
condition most likely to worsen under the wrong treatment.

---

## §2 the part that actually wears you down (M)

**Section heading:** Why melasma is different

Melasma is not a sun spot, and treating it like one is how most people make it worse. It is driven by
hormones as well as light, it recurs, and it rebounds hard when it is treated too aggressively.

It also fluctuates. It fades in winter, returns in summer, changes with pregnancy or a change of
medication, and that pattern is often what tells you it is melasma at all.

---

## §3 what a physician evaluation changes (M)

**Section heading:** Why this starts with an evaluation

- **It has to be correctly identified first.** Melasma, sun damage and post-inflammatory pigment look
  similar and respond very differently. The wrong call here is expensive and can set you back.
- **Hormonal and medication context matters.** Some medications aggravate melasma. A physician
  assessment covers that, which a device-led consultation does not.
- **Aggression is a risk, not a virtue.** Too much energy or too strong an active causes rebound
  darkening. The plan is deliberately conservative and long-horizon.

An evaluation determines whether a treatment is appropriate at all. It is not a safety or outcome
guarantee.

## §4 what the practice can look at (S)

**Section heading:** How melasma is approached here

Management rather than eradication, planned over months rather than sessions. Depending on what the
evaluation finds, this may involve carefully selected topical treatment, conservative light-based
options where appropriate, and disciplined daily sun protection, which is not optional and is the
part that decides the result.

Some presentations are better not treated with energy at all. That is a real outcome here.

## §8 questions people actually ask (M)

**Section heading:** Common questions

**Will it come back?**
Very likely, to some degree. Melasma is managed rather than cured, and maintenance is part of the
plan from the beginning rather than an upsell later.

**Is a laser the answer?**
Sometimes, and sometimes it is the problem. Energy-based treatment can trigger rebound pigmentation
in melasma. Whether it is appropriate for you is a physician decision.

**Does sunscreen actually matter that much?**
Yes. It is the single largest determinant of whether any melasma plan holds, and the plan says so
explicitly.

**What does it cost?**
Quote-only pricing. During your consultation, we will build a customized treatment plan with packaged
pricing.
