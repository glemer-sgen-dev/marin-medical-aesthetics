## §2 what the evaluation actually is (M)

**Section heading:** What the appointment covers

A comprehensive health evaluation, performed by a physician, before any treatment is discussed as
suitable for you. In the practice's own published words:

> as part of any treatment, she does a comprehensive health evaluation to minimize side effects and
> teaches clients how their actions can maximize and sustain the benefits they achieve.

That means the appointment covers your history, your medications, the condition in front of her, and
how the two interact. It is the part most practices skip, and it is the reason this one is organised
around it.

---

## §3 why an examination comes before a device (M)

**Section heading:** Why not just book the treatment

Because the treatment is the answer to a question nobody has asked yet.

Two people with what looks like the same concern often need different things, and sometimes one of
them needs nothing at all. Choosing the device first means you find that out afterwards, having
already paid for it. Choosing the evaluation first means the device is a conclusion rather than a
starting assumption.

An evaluation determines whether a treatment is appropriate at all. It is not a safety or outcome
guarantee.

---

## §4 what you leave with: the written plan (M)

**Section heading:** The written plan

You leave with a plan in writing. It says what was assessed, what is recommended, in what order, and
what to expect between appointments. It also says what to stop doing where that matters.

It is yours to keep, to think about, and to take elsewhere for a second opinion if you want one. A
plan you cannot take away is not a plan; it is a quote.

## §5 the evaluation covers function, not only appearance (M)

**Section heading:** Function counts as well

The evaluation includes concerns most aesthetics practices do not ask about: pelvic-floor strength,
continence, and comfort. These sit in the same appointment, in the same register, as skin and
contour.

They are among the most common reasons people book and among the least often raised out loud, which
is precisely why this practice raises them first.

## §6 what this practice does and does not do (S)

**Section heading:** What is and is not offered

**Offered:** physician evaluation, and the non-surgical treatments that follow from it across skin,
face and structure, body, and function.

**Not offered:** surgery of any kind, including facelift, eyelid surgery and liposuction. Medical and
surgical dermatology, including Mohs surgery. Skin-cancer treatment. If what you need is one of
those, the evaluation says so and points you toward it rather than substituting something else.

## §8 questions people ask before booking (M)

**Section heading:** Before you book

**Do I need to know what treatment I want?**
No. Arriving with a concern rather than a device name is the better starting point, and it is how
this site is organised.

**Will I be treated on the day?**
Not necessarily, and not as a default. The evaluation comes first. Where a treatment is appropriate
and you want to proceed, that is discussed as part of the plan.

**What if nothing is appropriate?**
Then you will be told that. It is a real outcome of an evaluation and not a failed appointment.

**Who performs the evaluation?**
Dr. Chris Bacchi, MD. She is the physician making the clinical decision.

**What should I bring?**
A list of your current medications and skincare products, and any history relevant to the concern.
The products matter more often than people expect.
