## §1 hero (S)

**H1:** Hyperpigmentation in San Anselmo

Patches of deeper colour sitting unevenly against the surrounding skin. It is a category rather than
a single condition, which is why the first useful step is working out which kind you have.

---

## §2 the part that actually wears you down (M)

**Section heading:** Why one product rarely fixes it

Hyperpigmentation covers several different processes: pigment left behind after inflammation, pigment
driven by hormones, and pigment driven by sun. They look alike in the mirror and respond very
differently to treatment.

That is why a product that worked for a friend often does nothing for you, and occasionally makes it
worse. It is also why people accumulate a drawer of half-used brightening serums.

---

## §3 what a physician evaluation changes (M)

**Section heading:** Why this starts with an evaluation

- **The type gets named.** Post-inflammatory pigment, melasma and sun-driven pigment need different
  plans, and melasma in particular can rebound under the wrong one.
- **The trigger gets addressed.** Pigment following breakouts or irritation keeps returning until the
  thing causing the inflammation is dealt with.
- **Skin tone changes the risk.** Deeper skin tones carry a higher risk of pigment change from
  treatment itself, which is a physician judgement rather than a device setting.

An evaluation determines whether a treatment is appropriate at all. It is not a safety or outcome
guarantee.

## §4 what the practice can look at (S)

**Section heading:** How hyperpigmentation is approached here

The type decides the plan. Depending on what is found, it may involve topical treatment, carefully
chosen light-based options where the pattern suits them, and a protection routine that prevents the
result unwinding. Where the pattern is melasma, the approach is deliberately more conservative.

## §8 questions people actually ask (M)

**Section heading:** Common questions

**Are the marks left by spots the same as melasma?**
No. Post-inflammatory pigment follows a specific injury or breakout and usually fades on its own
timeline. Melasma is patterned, hormonally influenced and recurrent. The plans differ substantially.

**Why did a brightening product make it worse?**
Irritation causes pigment. A product strong enough to irritate can deepen exactly what you are trying
to lift, particularly in deeper skin tones.

**Does skin tone affect what can be used?**
Yes, significantly. It affects both the choice of treatment and the settings, and it is one of the
clearest reasons this is a physician decision.

**What does it cost?**

pricing.
