## §2 the part that actually wears you down (M)

**Section heading:** The problem that is not what it is sold as

Cellulite is structural. Fibrous bands tether the skin to deeper tissue while fat pushes up between
them, which produces the dimpling. It is not a fat problem, not a fitness problem, and not a hygiene
or circulation problem.

That matters because the entire consumer market for it is built on the opposite premise. Creams,
brushes and wraps address a mechanism that is not the one causing it.

---

## §3 what a physician evaluation changes (M)

**Section heading:** Why this starts with an evaluation

- **The mechanism gets named.** Once you know it is a tethering problem rather than a fat problem,
  most of what is marketed for it stops being plausible.
- **Skin quality is assessed separately.** Laxity over the same area changes how the dimpling reads
  and is treated differently.
- **Expectations get set to improvement, not clearance.** Cellulite is reduced in appearance. It is
  not removed.

An evaluation determines whether a treatment is appropriate at all. It is not a safety or outcome
guarantee.

## §8 questions people actually ask (M)

**Section heading:** Common questions

**Will losing weight get rid of it?**
Usually not. Cellulite is present across every body size, including in people who are lean and fit.

**Do creams work?**
They address a mechanism that is not the one causing it. That is the honest answer, and it is why
this page explains the structure before discussing any treatment.

**Can it be removed?**
No. Appearance is improved. Any practice offering elimination is describing something other than
cellulite.

**What does it cost?**

pricing.
