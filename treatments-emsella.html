Non-invasive pelvic-floor strengthening. You stay fully clothed and seated for the session.

What it costs

What people come in with

Leaking with a cough, a laugh, a sneeze or a lift. Sudden urgency and not always getting there. Pelvic-floor weakness after childbirth, surgery or menopause, and the daily planning that builds up around it.

Most people have already tried pelvic-floor exercises, often for a long time, and either could not tell whether they were doing them correctly or found they did not go far enough.

What changes

How treatment here is decided

  • The evaluation covers function as well as appearance. Continence and pelvic-floor strength are assessed in the same appointment as everything else, not treated as a specialist add-on. A comprehensive health evaluation forms part of any treatment here.
  • It ends in a written plan you keep. What was assessed, what is recommended, and in what order.
  • It decides whether a treatment is appropriate at all. For some presentations the answer is a referral rather than a device, and that is a real result of the appointment.

An evaluation determines whether a treatment is appropriate at all. It is not a safety or outcome guarantee.

How it is approached

What a session is like

You sit, fully clothed, on the device for the session. It produces contractions in the pelvic-floor muscles well beyond what voluntary effort achieves. There is no undressing, no catheter, no anaesthetic and no recovery period, so you can return to the rest of your day afterwards.

It is delivered as a course rather than a single session. The number and interval are set at the evaluation.

Who this is for

Who it suits, and what is claimed

This practice's patients are typically adults roughly 35-65 across Marin County who want visible improvement in skin, body contour or pelvic-floor function, and who want a physician evaluating whether a treatment is appropriate for them at all.

No success rate, percentage or patient count is published here. Manufacturer figures are not this practice's figures, and no outcome figure has been verified for this practice, so none is claimed. What is published is the practice's real review corpus, which you can read and check for yourself.

The ceiling

What this is not

  • Not a substitute for medical investigation. Pain, blood, or a sudden change in symptoms need proper assessment first, and the evaluation will say so.
  • Not a cure, and not a guarantee that leaking stops.
  • Not surgery, and not presented as an alternative to a surgical repair where that is what is indicated.
  • Not a replacement for pelvic-floor exercise, which usually remains part of the plan.

Before you book

Common questions

01

Do I have to undress?

No. You remain fully clothed and seated throughout.

02

What does it feel like?

Strong contractions and a tingling sensation. It is not described as painful, though it is unfamiliar for the first minutes.

03

How many sessions will I need?

It is a course. The number and spacing are set at your evaluation rather than advertised in advance, because they depend on what is found.

04

Is there any downtime?

None is expected. People generally return to normal activity immediately.

05

Who is it not suitable for?

There are contraindications, including certain implanted metal and electronic devices, and pregnancy. Screening for those is part of the evaluation.

06

What does it cost?

Quote-only pricing. During your consultation, we will build a customized treatment plan with packaged pricing.
