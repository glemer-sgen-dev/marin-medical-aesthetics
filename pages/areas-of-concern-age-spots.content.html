Areas of concern

Age spots in San Anselmo

Discrete brown marks, usually flat, most often on the face, chest and backs of the hands. They sit where the sun reached most often over the longest time.

Why they bother people

Why they bother people more than the size suggests

They are small, and they are the thing people notice in photographs. They also appear on the hands and chest, which makeup does not practically cover, so they are the part of ageing that is hardest to manage privately.

What changes

Why this starts with an evaluation

A brown mark is not automatically an age spot. Some are melasma, which responds badly to treatments that suit age spots. Some warrant assessment for other reasons.

Anything suspicious is referred rather than treated. Skin-cancer treatment is not offered here, and a mark that has changed is not a cosmetic problem until someone qualified says it is.

The hands and chest are not the face. They heal differently and are treated with different settings and expectations.

An evaluation determines whether a treatment is appropriate at all. It is not a safety or outcome guarantee.

How it is approached

How age spots are approached here

Where appropriate, light-based treatment directed at the pigment itself, such as Forever Young BBL, ClearSilk or Halo laser, planned around the area being treated. Marks typically darken before they clear, which is expected and is explained before you agree to anything rather than after.

Who this is for

Who tends to come here for this

Adults roughly 35-65 across Marin County who want visible improvement in skin, body contour or pelvic-floor function, and who want a physician evaluating whether a treatment is appropriate for them at all.

The ceiling

What is not claimed here

  • No permanence. New marks form with further exposure.
  • No outcome guarantee, no "typical results" framing.
  • No before and after photographs, because none is published with a documented patient release yet.

No skin-cancer screening or treatment. Suspicious lesions are referred.

Before you book

Common questions

01

Why do they get darker before they fade?

That is the expected response when pigment is targeted. It is explained in advance, along with how long it usually lasts, so it is not a surprise on day three.

02

Can the hands be treated?

Often, yes, and they are one of the more common requests. They are planned differently from the face.

03

Will they come back?

New ones form with further exposure. Sun protection is part of the plan, not an afterthought.

04

Should I get a spot checked first?

If it has changed in size, shape or colour, yes, and by someone who treats skin cancer. That is not offered here.
