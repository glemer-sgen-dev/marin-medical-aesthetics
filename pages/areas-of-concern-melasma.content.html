Melasma in San Anselmo

Patchy, often symmetrical darkening across the cheeks, forehead or upper lip. It is the pigment condition most likely to worsen under the wrong treatment.

Why it is different

Why melasma is different

Melasma is not a sun spot, and treating it like one is how most people make it worse. It is driven by hormones as well as light, it recurs, and it rebounds hard when it is treated too aggressively.

It also fluctuates. It fades in winter, returns in summer, changes with pregnancy or a change of medication, and that pattern is often what tells you it is melasma at all.

Why this starts with an evaluation

It has to be correctly identified first. Melasma, sun damage and post-inflammatory pigment look similar and respond very differently. The wrong call here is expensive and can set you back.

Hormonal and medication context matters. Some medications aggravate melasma. A physician assessment covers that, which a device-led consultation does not.

Aggression is a risk, not a virtue. Too much energy or too strong an active causes rebound darkening. The plan is deliberately conservative and long-horizon.

An evaluation determines whether a treatment is appropriate at all. It is not a safety or outcome guarantee.

How it is approached

How melasma is approached here

Management rather than eradication, planned over months rather than sessions. Depending on what the evaluation finds, this may involve carefully selected topical treatment such as Obagi SkinTrinsiq or a medical facial, conservative light-based options where appropriate such as ClearSilk, and disciplined daily sun protection, which is not optional and is the part that decides the result.

Some presentations are better not treated with energy at all. That is a real outcome here.

Adults roughly 35-65 across Marin County who want visible improvement in skin, body contour or pelvic-floor function, and who want a physician evaluating whether a treatment is appropriate for them at all.

The ceiling

What is not claimed here

  • No cure. Melasma is managed and it recurs. Anyone promising permanent clearance is not describing melasma.
  • No outcome guarantee, no "typical results" framing.
  • No before and after photographs, because none is published with a documented patient release yet.
  • No claim that any device is safe for every skin type. Melasma treatment carries real risk of making things worse, and that is stated here rather than buried.

Before you book

Common questions

01

Will it come back?

Very likely, to some degree. Melasma is managed rather than cured, and maintenance is part of the plan from the beginning rather than an upsell later.

02

Is a laser the answer?

Sometimes, and sometimes it is the problem. Energy-based treatment can trigger rebound pigmentation in melasma. Whether it is appropriate for you is a physician decision.

03

Does sunscreen actually matter that much?

Yes. It is the single largest determinant of whether any melasma plan holds, and the plan says so explicitly.
