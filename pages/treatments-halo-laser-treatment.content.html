What people come in with

Skin where several things have changed at once:

  • Tone is uneven.
  • Texture is rough.
  • Pores are more visible.
  • Years of sun are showing.

Individually none of it is dramatic; collectively it reads as tired. This is usually the point at which gentler treatments have produced some improvement but not enough.

Raking daylight. It is the condition in which all four of those are actually noticed.

What changes

How treatment here is decided

  • The evaluation covers function as well as appearance, and includes whether your skin and your schedule suit a treatment with real recovery. A licensed MD performs it.
  • It ends in a written plan you keep, including what the recovery week actually looks like.
  • It decides whether a treatment is appropriate at all, and for some skin types and some pigment patterns the answer here is no.

An evaluation determines whether a treatment is appropriate at all. It is not a safety or outcome guarantee.

How it is approached

What a session is like

Numbing cream first, then the laser is passed over the treatment area. Skin is hot and red afterwards, and over the following days it darkens, roughens and flakes before revealing the new surface underneath.

That sequence is normal and it is not a complication.

You are told about it in detail before you book rather than discovering it on day three.

Who it suits, and what is claimed

This practice's patients are typically adults roughly 35-65 across Marin County who want visible improvement in skin, body contour or pelvic-floor function, and who want a physician evaluating whether a treatment is appropriate for them at all.

No outcome figures and no before and after photographs are published, because none is on file with a documented patient release.

The Medical Board of California requires an appropriate prior examination where prescriptive drugs and devices will be used, and that examination may not be delegated to registered nurses. That is why the evaluation comes first here, stated as the reason the process exists rather than as a safety claim.

The ceiling

What this is not

Two of the four below are reasons someone is turned away rather than booked.

  • Not a no-downtime treatment. There is a visible recovery period and it is stated plainly.
  • Not suitable for every skin type or every pigment pattern. In melasma in particular, resurfacing can worsen the problem.
  • Not a facelift. It changes skin surface and quality, not structure or laxity.
  • No "typical results" framing and no outcome guarantee.

Before you book

Common questions

01

How much downtime is there?

Several days of visible recovery for most people. The specifics are set at your evaluation, and if your schedule cannot accommodate it, that is worth knowing before booking rather than after.

02

Does it hurt?

Numbing cream is used. The sensation is described as hot and prickling. It is not called painless.

03

Can I have it if I have melasma?

That is exactly the question the evaluation answers. Resurfacing can aggravate melasma, so the assessment comes first.

04

How many treatments?

Fewer than a gentle laser, sometimes one, sometimes a small series. It is set at the evaluation.

05

What does it cost?
