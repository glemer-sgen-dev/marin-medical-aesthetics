What people come in with

Three things that tend to arrive together and are hard to name separately:

  • Diffuse redness that foundation flattens rather than fixes.
  • Dullness.
  • Skin that looks tired in photographs without any single feature being wrong.

It is also frequently the request of people who want an improvement but cannot take visible recovery time, which historically forced a choice between doing nothing and doing something disruptive.

That second motive is a scheduling constraint rather than a clinical finding, and it is treated as one.

What changes

How treatment here is decided

  • The evaluation covers function as well as appearance, and decides whether this is the right tool for what you have. A licensed MD performs it.
  • It ends in a written plan you keep.
  • It decides whether a treatment is appropriate at all, including whether a gentler or a stronger approach is the honest recommendation.

An evaluation determines whether a treatment is appropriate at all. It is not a safety or outcome guarantee.

How it is approached

What a session is like

The laser is passed over the skin, warming it gradually. Most people describe it as a warm, tolerable sensation rather than a painful one.

Skin is usually slightly flushed afterwards and settles quickly.

A prepared tray. The session itself is the short part.

It is delivered as a series, because the change is cumulative rather than immediate.

Who this is for

Who it suits, and what is claimed

This practice's patients are typically adults roughly 35-65 across Marin County who want visible improvement in skin, body contour or pelvic-floor function, and who want a physician evaluating whether a treatment is appropriate for them at all.

No outcome figures and no before and after photographs are published, because none is on file with a documented patient release.

The Medical Board of California requires an appropriate prior examination where prescriptive drugs and devices will be used, and that examination may not be delegated to registered nurses. That is why the evaluation comes first here, stated as the reason the process exists rather than as a safety claim.

The ceiling

What this is not

  • Not a resurfacing treatment. It works gently and cumulatively; it does not do what an ablative treatment does.
  • Not a single-session fix, and not a treatment for deep textural scarring.
  • No "zero downtime" claim. Flushing afterwards is normal and is described honestly rather than denied.
