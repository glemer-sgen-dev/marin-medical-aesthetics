Areas of concern

Hyperpigmentation in San Anselmo

Patches of deeper colour sitting unevenly against the surrounding skin. It is a category rather than a single condition, which is why the first useful step is working out which kind you have.

Book a consultation

Why one product rarely works

Why one product rarely fixes it

Hyperpigmentation covers several different processes: pigment left behind after inflammation, pigment driven by hormones, and pigment driven by sun. They look alike in the mirror and respond very differently to treatment.

That is why a product that worked for a friend often does nothing for you, and occasionally makes it worse. It is also why people accumulate a drawer of half-used brightening serums.

The drawer is part of the assessment

What changes

Why this starts with an evaluation

The type gets named. Post-inflammatory pigment, melasma and sun-driven pigment need different plans, and melasma in particular can rebound under the wrong one.

The trigger gets addressed. Pigment following breakouts or irritation keeps returning until the thing causing the inflammation is dealt with.

Skin tone changes the risk. Deeper skin tones carry a higher risk of pigment change from treatment itself, which is a physician judgement rather than a device setting.

An evaluation determines whether a treatment is appropriate at all. It is not a safety or outcome guarantee.

How it is approached

How hyperpigmentation is approached here

The type decides the plan. Depending on what is found, it may involve topical treatment such as Obagi SkinTrinsiq, carefully chosen light-based options where the pattern suits them such as ClearSilk or Forever Young BBL, and a protection routine that prevents the result unwinding. Where the pattern is melasma, the approach is deliberately more conservative.

Who this is for

Who tends to come here for this

Adults roughly 35-65 across Marin County who want visible improvement in skin, body contour or pelvic-floor function, and who want a physician evaluating whether a treatment is appropriate for them at all.

The ceiling

What is not claimed here

  • No promise of even tone, and no cure.
  • No outcome guarantee, no "typical results" framing.
  • No before and after photographs, because none is published with a documented patient release yet.

No claim that any treatment is suitable for every skin type. For pigment, that claim is not merely marketing overreach; it is clinically wrong.

Before you book

Common questions

01

Are the marks left by spots the same as melasma?

No. Post-inflammatory pigment follows a specific injury or breakout and usually fades on its own timeline. Melasma is patterned, hormonally influenced and recurrent. The plans differ substantially.

02

Why did a brightening product make it worse?

Irritation causes pigment. A product strong enough to irritate can deepen exactly what you are trying to lift, particularly in deeper skin tones.

03

Does skin tone affect what can be used?

Yes, significantly. It affects both the choice of treatment and the settings, and it is one of the clearest reasons this is a physician decision.

04
