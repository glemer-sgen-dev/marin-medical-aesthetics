What it actually is

The problem that is not what it is sold as

Cellulite is structural. Fibrous bands tether the skin to deeper tissue while fat pushes up between them, which produces the dimpling. It is not a fat problem, not a fitness problem, and not a hygiene or circulation problem.

That matters because the entire consumer market for it is built on the opposite premise. Creams, brushes and wraps address a mechanism that is not the one causing it.

Aimed at the wrong mechanism

What changes

Why this starts with an evaluation

The mechanism gets named. Once you know it is a tethering problem rather than a fat problem, most of what is marketed for it stops being plausible.

Skin quality is assessed separately. Laxity over the same area changes how the dimpling reads and is treated differently.

Expectations get set to improvement, not clearance. Cellulite is reduced in appearance. It is not removed.

An evaluation determines whether a treatment is appropriate at all. It is not a safety or outcome guarantee.

How it is approached

How cellulite is approached here

Where appropriate, treatment aimed at the tissue itself and at the quality of the skin above it, planned as a series with a defined review point.

Before you book

Common questions

01

Will losing weight get rid of it?

Usually not. Cellulite is present across every body size, including in people who are lean and fit.

02

Do creams work?

They address a mechanism that is not the one causing it. That is the honest answer, and it is why this page explains the structure before discussing any treatment.

03

Can it be removed?

No. Appearance is improved and the underlying structure remains. Elimination is not what any of this does, and a claim of elimination is describing something other than cellulite.
