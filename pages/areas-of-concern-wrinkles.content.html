Why it changes

Why the same treatment stops working

A line that was once only there when you smiled becomes a line that is always there. At that point relaxing the muscle softens it but no longer removes it, because the skin itself has changed.

This is where people conclude the treatment has stopped working, when what has actually happened is that the problem changed category.

What changes

Why this starts with an evaluation

Static lines need a different plan. Muscle treatment alone plateaus once a line is etched in.

Volume and support get assessed too. Some lines are the visible edge of volume loss elsewhere and do not respond to being treated directly.

Sequence and expectation get set honestly. Etched lines usually soften rather than disappear, and that is said before you spend anything.

An evaluation determines whether a treatment is appropriate at all. It is not a safety or outcome guarantee.

Where the expectation is set, before anything is spent

How it is approached

How wrinkles are approached here

Depending on what the evaluation finds, a plan may combine treatment aimed at movement such as Botox or Dysport or Emface with work directed at the skin itself, such as resurfacing or collagen stimulation with Halo laser or Scarlet RF, in an order that gives each part a chance to do its job.

Common questions

01

Why does my treatment not work as well as it used to?

Usually because the line has become static. The muscle is still being relaxed; the etched crease in the skin needs something else in addition.

02

Can they be removed completely?

Established wrinkles soften. Removal is not a claim this practice makes, and where a result would need surgery to achieve, you will be told that rather than sold an approximation.

03

Is more product the answer?

Rarely, and it is the most common route to an over-treated appearance.
