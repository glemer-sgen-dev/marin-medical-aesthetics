Why the effort stalls

Working the core and not reaching it

Core work assumes the muscle responds to the signal. After pregnancy or abdominal surgery, or where the abdominal muscles have separated, that assumption often fails: the effort goes in and the muscle that needs recruiting is not the one doing the work.

The result is months of training with a stomach that does not change, and a reasonable conclusion that you are doing it wrong.

A reasonable conclusion, and the wrong one

What changes

Why this starts with an evaluation

Separation gets checked for

Abdominal separation changes what exercise is appropriate, and some standard core exercises make it worse.

Strength, skin and fat get separated

A stomach that will not flatten can be any of the three, or a combination, and they respond differently.

The pelvic floor is assessed with it

Core and pelvic floor work together, and treating one while ignoring the other is a common and expensive omission.

An evaluation determines whether a treatment is appropriate at all. It is not a safety or outcome guarantee.

How it is approached

How this is approached here

Where appropriate, treatment that stimulates the muscle directly rather than relying on voluntary recruitment, planned as a series. It supplements training rather than replacing it, and the plan says so.

Common questions

01

I train hard already. Why would this add anything?

Because the limiting factor may not be effort. Where a muscle is not being recruited properly, additional training does not fix the recruitment problem.

02

Can this repair abdominal separation?

No such claim is made. Separation is assessed, it changes what is appropriate, and significant separation may be a surgical question.

03

Do I still need to exercise?

Yes. This is not a substitute and is not offered as one.
