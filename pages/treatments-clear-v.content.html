What people come in with

Fine red or purple vessels around the nose and cheeks that concealer blurs without hiding, and small vascular marks that have been there long enough to feel permanent.

Most people arrive having spent years covering the marks rather than treating them.

What changes

How treatment here is decided

  • The evaluation covers function as well as appearance, and separates discrete vessels from general flushing, which need different treatment. A licensed MD performs the health evaluation itself.
  • It ends in a written plan you keep.
  • It decides whether a treatment is appropriate at all, including whether an underlying condition such as rosacea should be addressed alongside.

An evaluation determines whether a treatment is appropriate at all. It is not a safety or outcome guarantee.

How it is approached

What a session is like

The laser targets the blood within the vessel, which absorbs the energy and closes it. Individual vessels can respond quickly, sometimes within one session, though more diffuse patterns take a series.

Sensation is a brief snap. Some redness or slight bruising afterwards is normal.

Which of the two patterns you have is not something to judge in a mirror. It is established at the appointment.

Who it suits, and what is claimed

This practice's patients are typically adults roughly 35-65 across Marin County who want visible improvement in skin, body contour or pelvic-floor function, and who want a physician evaluating whether a treatment is appropriate for them at all.

No outcome figures and no before and after photographs are published, because none is on file with a documented patient release.

The Medical Board of California requires an appropriate prior examination where prescriptive drugs and devices will be used, and that examination may not be delegated to registered nurses. That is why the evaluation comes first here, stated as the reason the process exists rather than as a safety claim.

The ceiling

What this is not

  • Not a treatment for the underlying tendency. It closes the vessels you have; it does not stop new ones forming.
  • Not a substitute for vascular assessment where leg veins are accompanied by aching, swelling or heaviness.
  • Not permanent in the sense of preventing recurrence.
  • No outcome guarantee and no "typical results" framing. No surgery, no medical or surgical dermatology and no skin-cancer treatment is offered here.

Before you book

Common questions

01

How many sessions?

Discrete vessels can respond quickly. Diffuse patterns take a series. Which you have is established at the evaluation.

02

Will they come back?

Treated vessels do not reopen, but new ones can form, particularly with rosacea.

03

Are leg veins treated the same way?

Not identically, and leg veins carry a question about circulation that facial vessels do not. If your legs ache or swell, say so.
